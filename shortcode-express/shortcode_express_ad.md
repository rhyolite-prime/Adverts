# ShortCode Express Advert Implementation Plan

## Campaign: “Be Fluent”

**Deliverable:** A 40-second advert combining live action, product footage, and motion graphics.

**Audience:** Software engineers, fintech teams, banks, and businesses building USSD services.

**Creative idea:** Turn tangled application logic into a clear, connected workflow, then show that workflow reaching a customer on a basic phone.

**Tone:** Technically credible, energetic, and confident.

**Core proposition:** ShortCode Express brings visual flow design and a structured build-and-release workflow to USSD development, powered by Sapo DSL Engine.

## Visual and audio direction

- Begin with tight framing, muted colours, and cluttered code.
- At the product reveal, open the frame, brighten the palette, and replace clutter with clean, connected flow nodes.
- Use actual ShortCode Express brand colours and interface footage wherever possible.
- Start the soundtrack with a low rhythmic pulse, introduce tension during the engineering problem, then resolve into confident forward motion.
- Keep gestures natural and restrained. Show an experienced engineer dealing with complexity, not an incompetent developer.
- Use generic integration icons unless provider availability and logo permissions are confirmed.

## Detailed scene-by-scene script

### Scene 1 — A dial connects a customer

**Time:** 0:00–0:04

**Voiceover:**

> Africa moves money with a dial.

**Setting:** A small shop. A customer holds a basic mobile phone while a merchant waits across the counter.

**Action and gestures:**

- Close-up: the customer’s thumb enters a USSD short code and presses the call button.
- The customer leans forward slightly, watching the screen.
- The merchant gives a small, expectant nod.

**Camera:** Begin on an extreme close-up of the keypad. Tilt gently toward the screen as a simple menu appears:

```text
Welcome
1. Pay merchant
2. Check balance
```

**Animation:** A thin pulse travels outward from the phone. Match-cut the pulse into a blinking cursor on an engineer’s monitor.

**On-screen text:** **A simple dial. A vital connection.**

**Sound:** Tactile keypad clicks. A low, rhythmic musical pulse begins.

### Scene 2 — The engineering bottleneck

**Time:** 0:04–0:09

**Voiceover:**

> But while apps got frameworks, USSD got endless if-else.

**Setting:** An engineer’s workstation. The monitor shows deeply nested conditional logic.

**Action and gestures:**

- The engineer scrolls through a long function.
- Their eyes track between the code and a handwritten flow diagram.
- They briefly rub their forehead, then pause with both hands above the keyboard.

**Performance note:** Keep frustration restrained and recognisable, not exaggerated.

**Camera:** Over-the-shoulder shot of the monitor. Push toward the screen as indentation levels grow.

**Animation:**

- `if`, `else if`, and `else` blocks extend beyond the editor.
- Branches cross and overlap, forming a tangled diagram.
- Small labels appear around the tangle: **New menu**, **API error**, **Another change**.
- The cursor stops. Hold for a fraction of a second.

**On-screen text:** **More branches. More complexity.**

**Sound:** Typing accelerates. The music becomes briefly tense, then cuts cleanly.

### Scene 3 — The reveal

**Time:** 0:09–0:13

**Voiceover:**

> Meet ShortCode Express. Powered by Sapo DSL.

**Setting:** The same workstation, now showing the ShortCode Express visual builder.

**Action and gestures:**

- The engineer releases a small breath.
- Their posture straightens.
- One purposeful mouse movement replaces the hesitant scrolling.

**Camera:** Pull back slightly to create a more open, comfortable composition.

**Animation:**

- Tangled branches straighten into clean connector lines.
- Lines resolve into menu, condition, and integration nodes.
- The ShortCode Express wordmark appears.
- A smaller endorsement follows: **Powered by Sapo DSL Engine**.

**On-screen text:** **Visual USSD development.**

**Sound:** A crisp transition hit. The music shifts into confident forward motion.

### Scene 4 — From short code to working flow

**Time:** 0:13–0:21

**Voiceover:**

> Get a short code—or bring yours. Drag. Drop. Connect. Build USSD flows without writing code.

**Setting:** A full-screen product demonstration, with a small live-action inset of the engineer.

**Action and gestures:**

- The engineer selects the short-code setup path.
- They drag a Menu node onto the canvas.
- They add a Prompt node and connect it to a Condition.
- Their free hand makes a subtle connecting gesture as the route completes.

**Camera:** Use a readable screen capture, not a distant shot of a monitor.

**Animation choreography:**

1. Show two setup choices: **Get a short code** / **Bring an existing code**.
2. Transition to the builder.
3. On “Drag,” a menu node moves onto the canvas.
4. On “Drop,” it settles with a soft snap.
5. On “Connect,” a connector joins two nodes.
6. A phone simulator updates with the resulting menu.

**On-screen text:** **Design the journey. Not the boilerplate.**

**Sound:** Three restrained interface sounds, synchronised with “Drag. Drop. Connect.”

**Release gate:** Show an existing-code onboarding or porting path only if it is available and approved for advertising. Do not fabricate product screens that imply an unavailable capability is live.

### Scene 5 — Connect the services behind the menu

**Time:** 0:21–0:29

**Voiceover:**

> Connect mobile money, banking, SMS, and KYC through the plugin marketplace. Need something unique? Build your own plugin.

**Setting:** The workflow remains visible. An integration panel opens beside it.

**Action and gestures:**

- The engineer selects a mobile-money integration.
- They drag it into a payment journey.
- A second teammate leans into frame and points to the next step.
- The engineer nods and adds a custom integration node.

**Animation:**

- Clearly labelled tiles appear: **Mobile Money**, **Core Banking**, **SMS / OTP**, **KYC**.
- One selected tile snaps into the flow.
- A pulse travels through **Customer input → Integration → Result**.
- On “build your own plugin,” a node labelled **Your Plugin** joins the canvas.
- Secondary category labels appear briefly beneath the panel: **Vouchers · Commerce · Logistics**.

**On-screen text:** **Connect existing services. Extend with your own.**

**Sound:** Soft connection clicks. The musical rhythm gains momentum.

**Visual guardrails:**

- Use generic service icons unless specific provider integrations and logo permissions are confirmed.
- Custom plugin development may involve code; do not label that step “no-code.”
- Marketplace tiles and category labels must reflect available integrations, not imply that roadmap items already ship.

### Scene 6 — Engineering control, not just a canvas

**Time:** 0:29–0:35

**Voiceover:**

**Setting:** A fast, readable sequence of actual product screens.

**Action and gestures:**

- The engineer tests a menu option in the phone simulator.

- They open the session dashboard.
- A teammate gives a small approving nod.

**Animation choreography:**

| Spoken beat | Visual |
| --- | --- |
| Simulate. | Select an option on the simulated phone; the next menu appears. |
| Validate. | Show the build result and validation status. |

**On-screen text:** **Test clearly. Release deliberately. Stay in control.**

**Sound:** A precise sequence of subtle confirmation tones. Avoid alarms or visuals suggesting an actual production outage.

## Timing and narration

- Approximately 98 spoken words; target a confident 150–160 words per minute with brief pauses at the reveal and tagline.
- Validate the overall duration and each scene window with a timed voiceover read before locking animation. Scene windows are edit targets, not a substitute for recording-based timing.
- Start grounded, sharpen the frustration in “endless if-else,” then become assured rather than breathless after the reveal.
- If a scene is too dense, simplify its visuals or rebalance adjacent scene timings while keeping the final runtime at 40 seconds.

## Claims and product-readiness checks

The repository documents the visual builder, phone simulator, validated builds, releases, analytics, and rollback. These support the engineering-control sequence, but documentation alone is not proof of production readiness.

The short-code porting and plugin-marketplace claims originate from the campaign proposition and require release-owner confirmation before publication. The platform README also describes demo authentication and sandbox payment rails; do not present a sandbox transaction as evidence of a live financial integration.

If the proposed capabilities are not launch-ready, use these replacements and retime the voiceover:

- **Scene 4 voiceover:** “Start with a short code. Drag. Drop. Connect. Build USSD flows without writing code.” Show only the supported short-code setup path.
- **Scene 5 voiceover:** “Connect your APIs through the visual builder. Bring your services into the flow, and shape the journey around your business.” Show verified HTTP/API configuration, not an unimplemented marketplace or plugin-authoring interface.
- Show a payment-success example only with an approved working integration or clearly identified demonstration footage.
- Leave out the proposed “60%” statistic unless a credible, current source supports its exact scope and wording.
- Avoid unsupported throughput, guaranteed deployment-time, or scale claims.

## Production implementation checklist

### 1. Pre-production

- [ ] Approve the proposition, voiceover, tagline, and CTA.
- [ ] Verify launch availability of short-code acquisition, porting, marketplace, and integrations.
- [ ] Confirm the public website address and permitted provider logos.
- [ ] Record a timed scratch voiceover and adjust scene windows.
- [ ] Prepare a coherent sample USSD flow that connects the builder demonstration to the shop interaction.
- [ ] Prepare clean demonstration accounts and remove personal data, API keys, phone numbers, and secrets from capture screens.
- [ ] Book the shop location, workstation location, customer, merchant, engineer, and teammate.
- [ ] Prepare brand assets, phone props, screen graphics, and wardrobe.

### 2. Capture

- [ ] Film customer and merchant coverage for the opening and closing scenes.
- [ ] Film the engineer’s frustration, reveal, and collaboration gestures.
- [ ] Capture the visual builder, simulator, validation result, release action, analytics, and rollback control.
- [ ] Capture acquisition and integration screens only after capability approval.
- [ ] Record clean keypad and interface sound effects as needed.

### 3. Animation and edit

- [ ] Build the tangled-code-to-flow transition.
- [ ] Animate drag, drop, and connection actions in sync with narration.
- [ ] Animate service connections and the custom-plugin node only if approved.
- [ ] Assemble the five-beat engineering-control sequence.
- [ ] Create the logo reveal and final end card.
- [ ] Record the final voiceover and lock the edit to 40 seconds.
- [ ] Mix music and sound effects below narration for speech clarity.
- [ ] Provide accurate captions and crop-safe essential text.

### 4. Review and delivery

- [ ] Product owner approves every capability shown or spoken.
- [ ] Check that code, connector directions, menu options, and UI actions are technically coherent.
- [ ] Verify text legibility on a phone-sized display.
- [ ] Confirm there are no exposed credentials or personal data.
- [ ] Confirm website spelling, brand spelling, captions, and runtime.
- [ ] Export the primary 16:9 master and caption file.
- [ ] Create 9:16 and 1:1 adaptations by reframing shots and repositioning graphics, not by blindly cropping the master.

## Acceptance criteria

- Final master runtime is 40 seconds.
- The narrative clearly moves from customer access to engineering complexity, visual development, operational control, and customer outcome.
- The product name and “Powered by Sapo DSL Engine” are legible at the reveal.
- The final card clearly presents “ShortCode Express,” “Be fluent,” and the approved CTA.
- All feature claims and depicted integrations are approved for the release being advertised.
- Narration is intelligible, captions are accurate, and essential graphics remain readable in each delivered aspect ratio.
